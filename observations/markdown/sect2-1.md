## Section 2.1 Q & A

1) If a star has an hour angle of 6 h, in which direction can you find it?

Answer: In the western sky; only due west on the horizon if the star is on the celestial equator.

2) When a star is on the observer ́s meridian, what is the local Siderial time?

Answer: When a star is on the observer's meridian, the local sidereal time is equal to the star's right ascension.

3) What is the hour angle, t, of the vernal equinox, Υ?

Answer: The hour angle of the vernal equinox is the local sidereal time.

4) What is the time called since an object passed the local meridian?

Answer: The time since an object has passed the local meridian is the hour angle. If it is 0, the object is on the local meridian. If it is positive, it has already passed it.

5) When is the sidereal time Θ = 0h 0m 0s?

Answer: The sidereal time is 0 when the vernal equinox is on the observer's meridian.

6) Where in the sky can you Lind the Sun at noon?

Answer: At noon, the Sun is at the observer's local meridian.

7) What is the declination of Polaris?

Answer: Since Polaris sits about 0.7° from the north celestial pole, the star's declination is about 89.3°.

8) Stockholm is located 3° east of the Swedish time meridian. This means that a star
is in the South
a. At the same time as on the time meridian
b. Earlier
c. Later

Answer: Since Stockholm is east of the Swedish time meridian, the Sun and the stars reach the meridian there earlier than on the time meridian, so the star is south earlier at Stockholm.

9) If a star has declination 30°, what is its height above the horizon at its highest
point? In which direction can you Lind it then?

Answer: A star reaches its highest point when it crosses the local meridian. For a star with declination 30°, you can find it looking exactly in the southern direction.

10) In which direction of the sky can you Lind a star at its lowest point

Answer: Since a star reaches its highest point due south, its lowest point in the sky is found due north.
